About Me

I am an Associate Professor of Experimental Pedagogy at the Faculty of Communication at IULM University. My academic background encompasses pedagogy, comparative psychology, evolutionary anthropology, and computer science. My research is focused on the use of advanced technological tools as educational and therapeutic mediators. My teaching and research activities concentrate on the application of digital technologies and social robots in education, including lifelong learning, as well as in the support of individuals with special needs.

In Italy, I have held positions at the Italian Institute of Technology in Genoa, the University of Macerata, and the University of Bergamo. At the University of Bergamo, I led a research project on integrating social robots into school curricula, with a strong emphasis on inclusion. At the University of Macerata, he contributed to the development of an enactive teaching approach supported by robots and various applications for using the robot Pepper in university courses. This work culminated in the publication of the book Social Robots for Enactive Didactics.

From 2014 to 2017, I worked at IIT as an Experienced Marie Curie Research Fellow. This Marie Curie project focused on the characteristics of human-robot social interaction, particularly the design of non-verbal communication behaviors for the iCub robot, including naturalistic gaze, eyelid movement patterns, and gestures. Prior to moving to Italy, I held positions at various universities in the United Kingdom and Japan, where I primarily focused on the application of social robots in teaching and care settings.

My main research interests, which connect experimental pedagogy with social robotics, human-robot interaction (HRI), and human-computer interaction (HCI), include robot-assisted teaching, educational technologies, robot-assisted therapy, human-machine interfaces, principles of social evolution, and enaction. I (co-)authored of over 80 publications on these topics.
